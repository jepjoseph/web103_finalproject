### Slide 1 — Introduction (25 seconds)

Hello everyone!

My name is X, and together with my teammate, Y, Z we're developing a web application called **Local Bloom**.

Imagine you're looking for a pottery workshop, an independent bookstore, or a small art supply shop in your neighborhood.

Where would you start looking?

You might search Google, browse Instagram, or ask friends for recommendations.

But what if you could discover all these creative places on one platform?

That's the idea behind Local Bloom.

### Slide 2 — The Problem (30 seconds)

Many independent creative businesses and community events struggle to gain visibility.

Unlike large commercial businesses, small creative spaces often depend on social media, flyers, and word of mouth to reach potential customers.

For people interested in creative activities, finding these places can be frustrating and time-consuming.

Information is scattered across different platforms, and it's not always easy to compare activities, locations, or prices.

We want to make that discovery process easier.

### Slide 3 — Our Solution (40 seconds)

Local Bloom is a community-driven web application designed to help people discover independent creative businesses and events.

Users will be able to explore art supply shops, pottery studios, independent bookstores, cafés, and creative workshops.

Our application will include search and filtering options based on activity type, distance, and budget.

Each location will have a dedicated details page showing useful information such as its address, operating hours, pricing, and available activities.

We'll also provide a management interface where organizers can create, update, and delete their listings.

This allows Local Bloom to serve both people looking for creative experiences and the businesses offering them.

### Slide 4 — User Experience (35 seconds)

Here's an example of how someone might use Local Bloom.

Imagine a student who wants to attend a pottery workshop but has a limited budget.

They open Local Bloom and search for pottery activities.

Next, they apply filters to find nearby workshops within their budget.

They can quickly preview a location using our slide-out modal or open its full details page.

After reviewing the workshop information, they can decide whether to visit.

Our goal is to make this entire process simple, intuitive, and accessible.

### Slide 5 — Technology and Feedback (45 seconds)

To build Local Bloom, we plan to use React and React Router for the frontend.

For the backend, we'll use Node.js and Express to create APIs that manage our application data.

We'll use PostgreSQL to store information about businesses, events, categories, and organizers.

We also plan to deploy our application using Render.

Our main development priorities will be the discovery experience, dynamic detail pages, and the ability to manage listings.

One challenge we're considering is how to implement distance-based filtering while keeping the project manageable.

We would appreciate feedback on whether our planned features are realistic within the remaining development time.

### Closing (15 seconds)

Ultimately, Local Bloom is about more than discovering places.

It's about connecting people with creative experiences and giving independent local businesses greater visibility.

**Discover locally. Create together. Grow with Local Bloom.**
