# RepairProof — Project Proposal

**CodePath WEB103 Final Project — Option B**

**Tagline:** Every repair. Every receipt. Every record.

## 1. Project Overview

RepairProof is a web application designed to help tenants and homeowners document home and appliance repairs, organize expenses, preserve supporting evidence, and prepare records for reimbursement requests or tax-related recordkeeping.

The platform provides a centralized place to record repair details, costs, dates, receipts, photographs, and repair history.

## 2. Problem Statement

Tenants sometimes pay for repairs to rental properties and later need to request reimbursement from their landlords. Without organized documentation, proving what was repaired, why it was necessary, and how much was spent can be difficult.

Homeowners also need reliable records of property maintenance and improvements for financial planning, property management, and potential tax-related purposes.

Receipts, invoices, and photographs are often scattered across phones, emails, and paper documents, making them difficult to retrieve when needed.

## 3. Description and Purpose

RepairProof helps users maintain an organized history of repairs to their homes and appliances.

Users can create repair records, describe problems, document work performed, record expenses, and attach supporting evidence.

Tenants can use these records to prepare reimbursement requests, while homeowners can maintain a history of property expenses and prepare supporting documentation for professional review.

RepairProof is a documentation and recordkeeping tool. It does not determine legal reimbursement rights or tax eligibility.

## 4. Inspiration

RepairProof was inspired by real-world situations in which tenants pay for necessary home repairs and struggle to organize evidence when requesting reimbursement from property owners.

The idea also addresses the difficulty homeowners face when tracking maintenance costs, preserving receipts, and gathering repair documentation for financial or tax-related purposes.

The goal is to make repair documentation easier, more organized, and accessible whenever users need it.

## 5. Target Users

- **Tenants:** Individuals renting homes who need to document repairs and expenses for possible reimbursement.
- **Homeowners:** Individuals who want to track property repairs, maintenance history, and expenses.
- **Property owners and landlords:** Potential future users who may review repair documentation and reimbursement requests.

## 6. Proposed Core Features

1. **Property Management:** Register and manage properties associated with repair records.
2. **Repair Records:** Create, view, update, and delete repair records.
3. **Expense Tracking:** Record repair costs, materials, labor, and payment details.
4. **Evidence Management:** Attach or reference receipts, invoices, and before-and-after photographs.
5. **Repair History:** Browse repairs organized by property and date.
6. **Search and Filtering:** Find repairs by category, date, property, or status.
7. **Reimbursement Preparation:** Organize eligible records into a reimbursement request summary without guaranteeing approval.
8. **Expense Summary:** View repair spending and prepare records for export or professional review.

## 7. Proposed Technology Stack

**Frontend:** React, JavaScript, HTML, CSS, React Router

**Backend:** Node.js, Express.js

**Database:** PostgreSQL

**Deployment:** Render

## 8. Proposed Custom Features

- Filtering and sorting repair records.
- Modal forms for creating or editing repair records.

## 9. Minimum Viable Product

The initial version will prioritize property management, repair CRUD operations, expense records, repair history, filtering, and basic evidence references.

Full document uploads, automated PDF exports, landlord accounts, and complex approval workflows can be evaluated as later enhancements.
